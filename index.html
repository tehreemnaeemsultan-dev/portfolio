01 / Onboarding

Value before the dashboard

The old onboarding asked for three things: credentials, an email code, and access to up to ten social accounts. Then it dropped users into an empty product.

I cut the steps that didn't earn their place and added one that gives something back. Paste your website, and the product builds your brand profile and writes your first posts.

My part: which steps went, which came inBuilt by: development and the AI team

Old flow

Onboarding is over.The dashboard is empty. Nothing is connected, nothing is written.

New flow

Product screenshots. The demo uses a public brand website and isn't affiliated with that brand. Personal details are blurred.

What I'd measure next: how many new users schedule a generated post, how many skip the URL step, and how the output holds up on single-page or non-English sites.

02 / Sample workspace

A brand that doesn't exist

A new account has no connected channels, no posts and no messages, so every module looked blank. Nobody could picture the product in use.

I made up a fashion label, Ellis & Co., and gave it one campaign that runs through Publisher, Inbox and Analytics. I chose the data to surface features people otherwise miss.

      03 / Brand Knowledge

      Built from sources, not typed

      The AI can only write in a brand's voice if it knows the brand. That knowledge used to sit in a settings form: a few colors and a voice description, typed by hand or left empty.

      I redesigned it so users add sources (a website, documents, past posts) and the product builds the profile. I prototyped the whole module in Lovable before it was built. The same profile now powers onboarding.

      My part: redesign and prototypeBuilt by: development
      Showing: after
      My prototype → what shipped

      What I'd measure next: how many workspaces have a complete profile, and how often people edit the generated fields, which shows how accurate the AI's first attempt is.

      04 / Navigation

      The top bar looked dated next to Sprout Social, Hootsuite and Metricool, which all use a sidebar. This was a visual update, not a fix for a measured problem.

      I prototyped the sidebar in Claude Design, got the PM's approval, and wrote the epic and user stories. It's live for all users.

      My part: research, prototype, epic and storiesBuilt by: development

      05 / Empty states

      Show the value, then ask

      Every analytics screen without a connected account showed the same thing: an old illustration and a Connect button. Nothing said what connecting would get you.

      I worked on a new pattern across the Analytics module. Each empty state now says what you'll be able to track, shows three or four cards with the specifics, and only then asks you to connect.

      06 / Side project

      A recruiting tool I built for our HR team

      I built this for ContentStudio's HR team: one place to find candidates, move them through hiring stages, email them and book interviews.

      It pulls public LinkedIn profiles by role and location, scores candidates against the role, sends outreach and rejection emails from the board, and lets candidates pick their own interview slot. When they pick, a Google Meet event is created and a confirmation goes out automatically.

      My part: built and deployed it myselfRuns on: Supabase, Vercel, Google Calendar, Zoho Mail

      Screenshots from the live tool. Candidate names, emails and staff details are blurred.

      What I'd measure next: time from first contact to interview, and how often candidates book their own slot instead of waiting for HR.
